Josh Tycko

About

I'm a postdoctoral fellow at Harvard Medical School, in Michael Greenberg's lab in the Department of Neurobiology and Martin Pollak's lab at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center. On an NIH F99/K00 fellowship, I develop protein barcoding methods to measure the cellular targets of secreted proteins at scale.

I earned my Ph.D. in Genetics at Stanford with Michael Bassik and Lacra Bintu, on an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship. There I built pooled screens that measure the functions of thousands of human protein domains, mapping transcriptional effectors and engineering compact tools to switch genes on and off.

At Stanford I was also a teaching assistant for a course on the science and bioethics of CRISPR, an instructor for a hands-on cell engineering lab, and a mentor to the iGEM team. As a BioFutures Fellow and co-leader of the Genetics Advocacy Committee, I worked with other trainees to study and improve graduate STEM training.

I also helped co-found Stylus Medicine, which is developing recombinase-based cell therapies for cancer.

Research

Interests

  1. Measure human protein function at scale

    I build pooled screens and other scalable synthetic-biology methods to read out the functions of many proteins and their gene-regulatory elements at once.

  2. Model how cells process information

    I use those measurements to work out how cells process information from molecular signals, including secreted proteins, and turn them into changes in cell state.

  3. Manipulate cells with new tools

    I turn what I learn into new tools, for example by engineering new proteins to write and regulate genetic programs. I focus on simplicity, specificity and deliverability, to make these tools safe and reliable enough to move towards becoming therapeutics.
